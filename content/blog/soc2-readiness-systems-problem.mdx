Teams usually start SOC 2 prep by looking for a template — a policy document
they can fill in and hand to an auditor. That gets you through a checklist,
not an audit. Auditors aren't grading your prose; they're sampling your
systems to see whether the policy is actually true.

## Start with access, not documents

The fastest way to fail an audit is to have a well-written access control
policy that doesn't match who can actually reach production. Before writing
anything down, we always answer:

- Who has standing access to production data, and why do they still have it?
- Is access granted through a system that logs the grant, or through a
  Slack message and a shared password?
- When someone leaves, is their access revoked automatically, or does it
  depend on someone remembering?

If the honest answers are uncomfortable, that's useful information — it's
also exactly what an auditor will find anyway, just later and more publicly.

## Logging you'll actually use

Compliance logging often gets built to satisfy an audit checkbox and then
never looked at again. The logging worth building is the kind your own team
would use to debug an incident at 3am — audit trail and operational value
should be the same system, not two separate ones.

Once access is scoped correctly and logging reflects reality, the actual
audit becomes a formality: you're just showing an auditor systems that were
already built to be true.
